The Problem

88 million kids log into Roblox every single day. But when parents try to find out if a specific game is safe for their child, they hit a wall. Roblox's own safety ratings are vague. Privacy policies are written for lawyers. Community forums are full of conflicting opinions.

The result? Parents either ban Roblox entirely — cutting their kids off from something their friends and cousins all enjoy — or they let them play with no real idea of what they're getting into. Neither option is good enough.

Meanwhile, the stakes keep rising. State attorneys general are suing Roblox for failing to protect children. COPPA 2.0 goes into effect April 2026. A major verification vendor exposed over 2,000 files including sensitive user data. Parents need better information — and they need it now.

Our Mission

SafeBlox translates the complexity of platform safety, privacy law, and game mechanics into plain-English answers that actually matter to parents. We don't tell you whether Roblox is safe. We tell you whether the specific game your child is playing is safe — and exactly what you can do about it.

What We Stand For

  • 🔍
    Transparency first
    Every rating is backed by documented evidence. Our full methodology is published and open for scrutiny. If a journalist or researcher wants to verify our work, they can.
  • 🚫
    Completely independent
    SafeBlox receives no payment from Roblox, any game developer, or any advertiser. We have no financial incentive to rate any game better or worse than it deserves.
  • 🗣️
    Plain English, always
    No lawyer-speak. No tech jargon. Parents are busy — we give them the information they need in the time they have.
  • 🔄
    Kept up to date
    Roblox games change constantly. We revisit ratings regularly and update them when a game's risk profile shifts.
  • 🔒
    Privacy by design
    We don't ask for your child's name, account, or any personal information. We never will.
